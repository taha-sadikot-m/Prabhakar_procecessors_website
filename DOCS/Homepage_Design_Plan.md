## THE CORE CONCEPT

> **The website IS the fabric — not a website about fabric.**

Every other textile company website shows photos of fabric and puts text next to them.

This design does something different: **the screen is a loom**. The user's scroll is the machine pulling the fabric through. The entire page is one continuous piece of cloth — raw at the top, transformed and radiant by the bottom.

The visual soul is Indian — warm ivory light, festival color, the weight of craft and tradition — not cold or industrial. Think the color and warmth of a Surat textile market on a bright morning, not a European factory floor.

The user doesn't browse a website. They **witness a process**.

## INDIAN ACCENT DESIGN LANGUAGE

This is what makes this site unmistakably Indian — not a Western industrial site, not a generic "global" site — but rooted in the culture of Surat, Gujarat, and India's textile heritage.

### Indian Motif Library

**1. Rangoli Dots** — Used as:
- Section transition reveals (instead of a plain color sweep, dots expand outward in a rangoli pattern as new sections enter)
- Corner decorations on full-bleed sections
- Background pattern on the trust section (very faint, cream-on-ivory)

**2. Paisley / Kalka (Boteh)** — The teardrop motif that originated in India. Used as:
- Pull-quote border frames
- The fabric "hole" on each swatch card (instead of a plain punched circle, it's a paisley-shaped cutout)
- Decorative element in section transitions

**3. Kantha Stitch Lines** — The running stitch embroidery from India. Used as:
- Section dividers — instead of a solid line, a hand-stitched looking dashed line in saffron or peacock teal
- Animated on scroll-in: the stitch "draws itself" from left to right

**4. Jali (Lattice) Pattern** — Intricate geometric cutwork from Indian architecture. Used as:
- Very faint background texture on the Scale section (behind the big indigo numbers)
- Overlay mask on images — images are seen through a jali pattern for a split second during transitions

**5. Block Print / Ajrakh Pattern** — The geometric resist-print tradition of Gujarat (Kutch). Used as:
- Swatch textures for printing services
- Hero section edge decoration (thin border on two sides — top and left)
- The footer divider line is an ajrakh repeat motif

**6. Ikat Weave** — The feathered zigzag of Indian ikat textiles. Used as:
- Brand Story section background texture (very faint, warm)
- Animated: threads lock into ikat pattern when the weave animation resolves

**7. Zari / Gold Thread** — The metal thread woven into Indian silk. Used as:
- Gold accents throughout — the `+` in statistics, borders, the thread needle cursor tip
- The thread navigation line shimmers gold when hovering a section tag

**8. Lotus Motif** — India's national flower, also a symbol of purity and craftsmanship. Used as:
- Quality/certification seals on the swatch cards (small stamp)
- The "period" dot in section numbers: instead of `01.` → a tiny lotus symbol `❁ 01`

## TYPOGRAPHY SYSTEM

**Three typefaces. Three voices. Warm, not cold.**

### 1. DISPLAY / HEADLINES — Warm Condensed Serif
- Font: **Cormorant Garamond** (free, Google) — tall, elegant, warm, has historical depth without feeling old
- OR: **Playfair Display Condensed** — editorial, warm, Indian design studios use it frequently
- Use: Major headlines, hero text, section statements
- Style: Mix of ALL CAPS for statements, sentence case for human moments
- Size: 8vw to 30vw
- Color: Deep warm brown (#2D1B0E) on light backgrounds; ivory (#FFF8F0) on dark/indigo

### 2. BODY — Warm Humanist Sans
- Font: **DM Sans** or **Plus Jakarta Sans**
- Use: Descriptions, labels, explanations
- Style: 15–16px, line-height 1.75, slightly warm — not cold or clinical
- Color: #4A3728 (medium warm brown) — never pure black on light backgrounds

### 3. DATA / NUMBERS — Technical Monospaced
- Font: **IBM Plex Mono**
- Use: Statistics, production numbers, meter counts, location data
- Style: Feels like a factory measurement but with warmth
- Color on indigo sections: #D4AF37 (Indian gold) for the numbers themselves

### 4. INDIAN SCRIPT ACCENT (Optional, high impact)
- For one or two key moments, a Devanagari or Gujarati script phrase appears — not for navigation, but as pure visual texture and cultural pride
- Example: In the Brand Story section, very faintly behind the number "17" — the word **"सूरत"** (Surat) in a Devanagari display font, cream-on-ivory, barely visible — a hidden layer for those who see it

## THE NAVIGATION — "THE WARP THREAD"

Forget the top nav bar entirely.

**What it is**: A single thread (3px) that runs the full height of the left edge of the screen. Saffron orange (#F7941D). The warp thread of the fabric — the backbone of the loom.

**How it works**:
- Thread starts at the top as a light neutral (#D4CFC8 — raw undyed)
- As the user scrolls, the thread FILLS with saffron from top downward — the dye moving up the thread
- At section transition points, a small horizontal tag is attached — like a quality control tag on a real spool
- Tags are small cream paper tags in an angled label style: `❁ 01 HOME` / `❁ 02 STORY` / `❁ 03 PROCESS` etc.
- Clicking a tag snaps the user to that section
- At the deep indigo Scale section, the thread turns gold (#D4AF37) — zari thread, the most precious
- On mobile: horizontal progress thread at the bottom of the screen

## CURSOR — "THE THREAD NEEDLE"

The cursor is replaced by a custom needle cursor.

- Shape: A thin elongated needle SVG — gold-tipped, warm silver body
- Behind it: a saffron thread trail (2px) that follows the cursor path, fades after 400ms
- On light sections: the trail is saffron (#F7941D)
- On the deep indigo section: the trail becomes ivory/gold
- On hover over interactive elements: the needle "eyes" — a small threading animation

## PAGE LOADER

Duration: 2.5 seconds. No percentage spinner.

**Animation sequence**:
1. Screen is warm ivory (#FFF8F0) — not black. Bright from the first frame.
2. From the top, saffron warp threads drop down vertically, staggered — 12–15 threads, like a traditional handloom being set up
3. A single deep indigo weft thread weaves left to right through the warp threads — the classic over-under loom motion
4. Where the weft crosses warp, the word **PRABHAKAR** materializes in deep indigo — as if woven into existence, letter by letter
5. Below it: **PROCESSORS PVT LTD** — in smaller warm brown caps, same weave-in animation
6. Pause 0.5s — a small rangoli dot pattern blooms briefly at the four corners of the screen (subtle, 0.3s)
7. The entire loom structure rises upward — pulling the fabric up to reveal the hero

## SCROLL & TRANSITION BEHAVIOR

### Between Sections:
- NOT hard-line background color changes
- Each transition: rangoli dot pattern expands from a corner, revealing the next section behind it
- Achieved with SVG clip-path animation — dots expand outward in a radial rangoli formation

### Section Entry Animations:
- Text elements: "unspool" from a left anchor point — thread pulled from a spool, left to right
- Numbers: grow from a center-point outward — like dye spreading from a drop
- Kantha stitch lines: draw themselves left-to-right on entry (SVG stroke-dashoffset)
- Swatch book: cards flutter in one by one with a spring physics feel

### Scroll Speed:
- Light parallax on the hero dye bloom vs. foreground text — 2 layers only, subtle
- No aggressive effects — the animations serve the story, not the other way around
